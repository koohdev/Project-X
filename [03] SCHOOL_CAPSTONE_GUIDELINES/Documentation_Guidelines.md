## 📝 10.2 Documentation Content Requirements

### Title Page

- Title must be **concise, descriptive, and comprehensive**
- Begin with a **noun** or **present participle** form of an action verb
- State in **general terms** to bundle problem areas
- ❌ **NO humorous or catchy titles** — this is discouraged

---

### Adviser's Recommendation Sheet

- Must be **duly signed** by the capstone adviser
- No final documentation accepted without this signature

---

### Dean's Acceptance Sheet

- Must be **duly signed** by the Dean
- Provides space for Dean's signature indicating acceptance

---

### Panel's Approval Sheet

- Must be **duly signed by ALL defense panelists**
- Indicates panel's acceptance of the work

---

### Abstract

**Requirements:**
- **150-200 words** only
- Short, direct, and complete sentences
- States the **rationale** and **objectives** of the project
- Must be informative enough to substitute for reading the full paper

**Restrictions:**
- ❌ NO citations or quotes
- ❌ Do NOT begin with "This paper/document/project/study..."

---

## 📚 Chapter Content Guidelines

### Chapter 1.0 Introduction

#### 1.1 Project Context

**Requirements:**
- Introduce the problem — what it is all about
- Describe the **existing and prevailing problem situation** (global → national → regional → local)
- Provide **strong justification** for selecting the research problem
- State the link between rationale and proposed research problem
- **Minimum 2 pages** of presentation and discussion

**Guide Questions to Address:**
- What is the function of the project?
- What is good in your project?
- What makes your project unique, innovative, and relevant?

**Must Include:** Discussion of relevant topics, concepts, and technologies to be utilized.

#### 1.2 Objectives

##### 1.2.1 General Objective
- **Single paragraph** describing the general objective

##### 1.2.2 Specific Objectives
- List of specific work to accomplish the general objective
- All objectives must be **SMART:**

| Criterion | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **S**pecific | Problem specifically stated |
| **M**easurable | Easy to measure using research instruments |
| **A**chievable | Solutions are feasible |
| **R**ealistic | Results gathered scientifically, not manipulated |
| **T**ime-Bound | Time frame required for each activity |

#### 1.3 Scope and Limitations

**Scope:** Extent of prototype and means of evaluation on capability of solving the problem.

**Limitations:** Conditions beyond control that may restrict conclusions and application. **Justify each limitation.**

### Chapter 2.0 Review of Related Literature/Systems

**Two Components:**

#### Related Theories
- Start with an **anchor theory**
- Supporting theories elaborate the anchor theory
- Proper endnoting/footnoting following correct bibliography
- Maintain fluidity and continuity

#### Related Projects/Systems
- Overview of current system/project
- Inventory of related and existing projects
- **Comparative matrix** recommended
- **Screenshots** for believability
- Include **3-6 related studies/projects**
