## Before vs. After Comparison

### Paragraph 1 (GBL Definition and Connection)

**Before:**
> Game-Based Learning (GBL) is a teaching approach that uses game mechanics like points, levels, and rewards to teach academic content through gameplay instead of traditional instruction (Prensky, 2001) [6]. The idea is that students learn the material as a result of playing the game, not by sitting through lectures or drills. Studies have shown that GBL has a positive effect on both test scores and student motivation among primary school learners [6]. GBL is the anchor theory for this study because the whole design of *Chronicles of Arithmos* is built around it. The system uses RPG combat, exploration, and character progression to teach Grade 4–6 arithmetic.

**After:**
> Game-Based Learning (GBL) introduces academic content through mechanics like levels and points, replacing conventional instruction [6]. Instead of absorbing information through static lectures, the student acquires knowledge organically during active gameplay. Prensky (2001) documented that this interactive approach yields higher assessment scores and stronger motivation among elementary learners [6]. This concept forms the foundational framework for *Chronicles of Arithmos*. By converting Grade 4–6 arithmetic exercises into fantasy exploration and role-playing combat scenarios, the application directly applies GBL principles to mathematics education.

### Paragraph 2 (Supporting Theories Intro)

**Before:**
> Three supporting theories build on GBL and explain specific parts of how *Chronicles of Arithmos* works. Mathematics Anxiety explains why a game-based approach is needed for this age group. Flow Theory explains how difficulty has to be managed to keep students engaged. Narrative-Centered Learning explains how tying lessons to a story helps students remember what they learned.

**After:**
> Beyond this core framework, three additional theories clarify the specific mechanisms of the proposed system. The concept of Mathematics Anxiety formally justifies why students in this demographic require a low-stakes environment. Managing player interaction relies on Flow Theory. This principle dictates how the software scales the difficulty of generated equations to maintain a balanced challenge between boredom and frustration. Finally, Narrative-Centered Learning explains the cognitive benefit of attaching abstract numbers to a persistent storyline.
