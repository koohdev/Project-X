# Fishbone Diagram (Ishikawa Cause & Effect Diagram)

### Overview

A **fishbone diagram**, also known as a **cause-and-effect diagram** or an **Ishikawa diagram** (developed by Kaoru Ishikawa), is a foundational tool in quality management and root cause analysis. It provides a visual framework to brainstorm, organize, and categorize the underlying factors contributing to a specific problem or outcome.

#### Structural Anatomy

* **The "Head":** The specific problem statement or effect being analyzed (placed on the right).
* **The "Spine":** The central horizontal arrow pointing directly to the problem statement.
* **The "Ribs" (Primary Bones):** Major categories of potential causes branching off the spine.
* **The "Sub-Branches" (Minor Bones):** Specific contributing causes and deeper sub-causes branching off each primary category.

---

### Key Benefits & When to Use

* **Root Cause Identification:** Moves teams beyond surface-level symptoms to uncover true root causes.
* **Bottleneck Discovery:** Visually maps out interdependencies across processes, equipment, and personnel to expose operational inefficiencies.
* **Prevention of Recurring Issues:** Fixing root causes prevents identical issues from re-emerging.
* **Team Collaboration:** Structures group brainstorming sessions so cross-functional team members can contribute diverse perspectives.
* **Change Impact Assessment:** Helps visualize how strategic or process changes might impact interconnected systems across multiple iterations.

---

### Standard Category Frameworks

The categories chosen for the primary ribs depend on the industry and the nature of the problem:

#### 1. The 6M Model *(Predominantly used in Manufacturing & Engineering)*

* **Manpower (People):** Personnel, human error, training, skills, workload, and ergonomic factors.
* **Machinery (Equipment):** Tools, hardware, material-handling systems, computers, and software.
* **Methods (Processes):** Standard operating procedures, workflows, instructions, and regulations.
* **Materials:** Raw materials, parts, supplies, vendor quality, and specifications.
* **Measurement:** Gauges, inspection criteria, laboratory analysis, and calibration routines.
* **Mother Nature (Environment):** Temperature, humidity, shop floor cleanliness, and external conditions.
* *(Optional 7th M)* **Money:** Operating expenses, capital expenditure, and budgetary constraints.

#### 2. The 4S Model *(Widely used in the Service Industry)*

* **Surroundings:** The physical environment, customer-facing areas, and external factors.
* **Systems:** Internal IT systems, communication channels, and procedural workflows.
* **Skills:** Employee training, competency levels, and task execution abilities.
* **Suppliers:** Vendors, contractors, partners, and external service providers.

#### 3. The 8P Model *(Used in Service, Retail, Administration & Marketing)*

* **Procedures:** Operational routines and steps required to deliver the service.
* **Policies:** Rules, regulations, organizational guidelines, and company decisions.
* **Place:** Locations, facilities, distribution channels, and operational footprint.
* **Product:** Design, quality, and characteristics of the goods or services offered.
* **People:** Staff, contractors, managers, and customer interactions.
* **Processes:** How activities and tasks flow from start to finish.
* **Price:** Cost structure, pricing strategy, fees, and value proposition.
* **Promotion:** Marketing campaigns, communication, and messaging clarity.

#### 4. The Simple / Custom Model

* Has no predetermined labels; the team defines 2 to 6 unique categories tailored specifically to the problem (e.g., *Product-related* vs. *Service-related* concerns).

---

### Specialized Diagram Variations

* **Cause Enumeration Diagram:** A comprehensive listing of all possible causes brainstormed and categorized after data collection.
* **Process Fishbone:** Organizes cause categories along the sequential steps of a workflow or production pipeline.
* **CEDAC (Cause and Effect Diagram with the Addition of Cards):** Uses movable sticky notes/cards for dynamic brainstorming and continuous improvement tracking.
* **Desired-Result Fishbone:** Used positively to map out factors required to achieve a target goal or successful outcome.
* **Reverse Fishbone Diagram:** Used to investigate how to prevent a potential failure mode before it happens, or to analyze how a solution might cause secondary issues.

---

### Step-by-Step Construction Procedure

1. **Define the Problem Statement (The Head):**
* Agree on a clear, concise statement of the problem (e.g., *"Frequent pump defects upon delivery"*).
* Write it inside a box on the right-hand side of a whiteboard or digital canvas and draw a central horizontal arrow (the spine) pointing to it.


2. **Establish Major Categories (The Main Ribs):**
* Select an appropriate framework (e.g., 6M, 4S, 8P, or custom).
* Draw diagonal lines branching off the central spine for each category.


3. **Brainstorm Potential Causes:**
* Ask the team *"Why does this happen?"* for each category.
* Write direct causes as secondary lines branching off the main category ribs.


4. **Uncover Sub-Causes Using the "5 Whys":**
* Drill deeper into each cause by repeatedly asking *"Why?"*.
* Add sub-causes as smaller branches off the secondary ribs to show causal relationships.


5. **Identify Multi-Category & Recurring Factors:**
* Note causes that appear across multiple ribs (e.g., *calibration errors* falling under both *Methods* and *Measurement*). Cross-category causes are often prime suspects for root issues.


6. **Evaluate, Prioritize, and Plan Action:**
* Analyze findings using data collection tools (e.g., check sheets, Pareto charts).
* Formulate corrective actions and assign ownership using a responsibility matrix or action plan.

### Practical Examples

#### Example 1: Grocery Store Bag Failures *(Simple/Retail Application)*

* **Problem Statement:** *Customers frequently drop grocery bags due to handle failures, causing product damage.*
* **Primary Category Identified:** *Materials / Equipment.*
* **Investigation:** The store recently replaced handled paper bags with non-handled bags to cut costs.
* **Root Cause Resolution:** Compare the cost savings of non-handled bags against customer dissatisfaction and broken-merchandise write-offs; determine whether to reinstate handled bags or train staff on safe bagging limits.

#### Example 2: Product Iron Contamination *(Manufacturing Application)*

* **Problem Statement:** *Periodic iron contamination in finished chemical products.*
* **Findings Across Categories:**
* **Machinery:** Specific exchangers, reactors, and pumps were evaluated for material degradation.
* **Methods & Manpower:** Iron maintenance tools were being used during sample collection and maintenance.
* **Measurement:** Analytical lab instruments exhibited periodic calibration drift.


* **Root Cause Resolution:** Replace ferrous maintenance tools with non-ferrous alternatives and implement a standardized calibration log (check sheet) for lab instrumentation.
